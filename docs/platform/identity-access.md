# Accounts, representation and authorization

A secretary can record a visitor without creating an account. An account proves the identity of a person using the service. Church membership, family ties, employment and permission to act for someone each have a separate owner. An organization is the isolation boundary in this domain. A person serving two independent organizations has a distinct local account in each, even when both accept the same sign-in identity. Switching organizations clears search suggestions, selected exports and act-as context.

## Logical records

Throughout this directory an unmarked field is required, a question mark after a type means optional, and an ID points to one record. Each row has its own opaque id and organization_id referencing Organization unless expressly shared. Code values are stable machine-independent terms with separately translated labels. An instant stores one point in time; a local date or local time has no implied time zone. Time windows include their start and exclude their end. Ref(T) is a typed foreign key; ResourceRef carries an allowed entity type and stable record id validated together. Lists denoted set are unique values or child rows. Version is a strictly increasing revision used for conflict detection. A secret reference identifies protected secret storage. A fingerprint detects change and does not substitute for authorization.

## Member entry and account recovery

1. On invitation, show the target congregation and request proof of control of the intended channel. A verified address shared by two adults or several children queues an identity review. Never auto-link on name, birth date or contact match alone. A person may ask for self-service access without an invitation and follows the same proof and review rules.
2. Accepting the invitation consumes it atomically and links one account. A repeated acceptance returns the established result to the verified claimant. A competing person claim requires staff resolution. Confirmation to the established channel states when the account changed and where to report an error.
3. Support a personally controlled sign-in method and a second independent factor for privileged operations. Support a recovery method accessible to someone who has lost a phone. Replacing a factor or changing the main contact requires recent proof and notice to the previously verified contact. Document the assisted recovery evidence, two-person approval for privileged accounts, delay and revocation of existing sessions.
4. Allow members to inspect and revoke their sessions. Require fresh proof for delegation, bulk export, payout-detail changes and financial approval. On a shared kiosk end the session, remove local personal content and return to the welcome screen after completion or timeout. Unclaimed invitations expire. Failed sign-in and recovery messages reveal no membership, account or household status.
5. An administrator suspends an account immediately after a reported compromise or staff departure. End sessions, invitations, role bindings as directed and delegated tokens. Preserve authored minutes and journal attribution. The person and membership remain available for authorized recordkeeping. Re-enable only after review.

## Decision order

Resolve identity and selected organization first. Resolve subject and effective instant, authorize action and resource scope, then apply class, field, purpose, representation and explicit restriction checks. A current explicit restriction wins over an ordinary role grant. Emergency access is a dedicated, reviewed exception to the specific rules marked overrideable. The same checks apply to direct record retrieval, counts, search results, attachments, exports, notifications, integrations, cached representations and offline assignment. Enforce obligations again when a queued job actually runs. Constrain updates to permitted fields. Decline the entire mutation when protected fields are submitted.

Members can update permitted contact details about themselves. A household delegate may register a named child while seeing only the permitted child fields. A choir leader receives their own current roster. Finance roles separate collecting, approving, posting and reconciling where configuration requires it. A staff administrator can manage roles only within a configured grant ceiling and cannot approve their own elevation. A support operator receives diagnostic metadata by default and requests time-bound access to congregation data separately.

When acting for someone, show the signed-in person, represented person, scope and expiry before submission. Log both identities; end representation immediately when the governing grant changes. A person may revoke optional delegates without deleting historic actions. Unknown ownership, ambiguous scope and stale grants fail closed. At membership changes and age transitions, issue a review task and expire conditional grants under the approved local policy.

## Acceptance

- Two adults share one mobile number. Verifying an invitation intended for one adult never reveals the other's profile or resets their sign-in method.
- A leader who serves one campus requests another campus's people by direct identifier, search, export and attachment preview. All four results reveal zero private fields or counts.
- A guardian can register one child while their spouse's donor statement and a second child's protected address remain inaccessible. Removing the grant stops a second registration under the same still-open session.
- A treasurer loses access after an export is queued. The download and underlying export job both recheck authorization and withhold the file.
- An emergency reader supplies a reason, sees only the named case for the permitted interval and produces a review task. The audit distinguishes a real emergency reader from an account impersonator.
