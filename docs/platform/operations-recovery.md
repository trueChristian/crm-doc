# Operating the register and recovering service

The system administrator manages technical health. Departmental custodians decide whether their data is complete, accurate and ready to reopen. A church names responsible and alternate people for access, finances, worship services, incident handling, member support and production recovery. Keep an emergency contact route and a paper service-day continuity procedure available outside the affected system.

## Service-day operations

Distinguish full, degraded, read-only and suspended operations. Specify what a secretary or check-in volunteer can still do when search, email, storage, authentication, payment processing or the whole service is down. Never report a provider-dependent action as completed until its authoritative result is known. When payments are uncertain, provide a receipt reference and keep the monetary transaction pending until reconciliation. When notices back up past event time, expire obsolete reminders and alert the organizer.

Measure availability for sign-in, roster lookup, check-in, donation receipt retrieval and online transaction acknowledgement. Track error ratio, response-time distribution, stale authorization signals, age of oldest unsent item, integration reconciliation variance, pending background jobs, backup freshness, capacity headroom and expiry of required credentials. Dashboards separate organization-specific and system-wide indicators. Diagnostic access is limited and time-bound. Member support can follow a correlation reference without seeing private payloads.

Set values according to service size and risk. A reference acceptance profile for a 2,000-person church uses 40 simultaneous service-desk users. During a 30-minute representative test, 95 percent of roster lookups must return within two seconds and confirmed check-ins within three seconds on the stated connection. An example core-register recovery target is four hours with at most fifteen minutes of data loss. Real installations record the selected objectives, test load, measured network conditions and exceptions. Published minutes, financial commitments and child safety decisions additionally require downstream reconciliation if any events could have been lost.

## Backup, restore and incidents

1. Automate consistent captures, verify manifest and integrity, maintain a separately protected recovery copy and separate access to keys. A completion message proves only capture; schedule actual restores. Alert on missing recovery keys, expired copies or gaps between the latest verified point and the loss target.
2. Restore into an isolated workspace. Disable outgoing communications, payment collections, publishing, directory visibility and external callbacks until reconciliation. Authenticate the operators, record the requested recovery point and confirm scope; restoring one church must never overwrite another's data.
3. Reapply durable revocations, suppressions, holds, erasure markers and post-recovery corrections before making restored records readable. Identify uncertainty since the recovery point. Compare external settlement identifiers, delivered message IDs, check-in evidence, inventory movements and issued membership changes to the restored position.
4. Custodians verify giving handoffs against external receipts by currency and designation, membership totals, sampled attachment digests, visibility, blocked child release, pending jobs and consent. Capture exceptions with a responsible owner and compensating action. The incident coordinator authorizes a staged reopening and publishes understandable status updates.
5. Coordinate incidents from triage through containment, recovery, closure and corrective action. Keep protected evidence with an assigned custodian. Record what was observed, affected people or services, notices authorized and decision times. Follow the approved local escalation calendar. A post-incident review assigns owners, dates and retests.
6. For releases, test representative upgraded legacy data, deployment interruption, queued jobs from the previous edition and failback. Declare the last safe rollback point. Irreversible migrations require backup, recovery rehearsal and a forward repair path. Inform departmental owners of changed behavior before activation.

## Acceptance

- A restore rehearsal of one congregation's permitted scope verifies counts and attached evidence, preserves tenant isolation, and emits zero messages or payment requests. The report compares measured time and loss against the signed objectives.
- A previously erased address appears in an older backup. Reopening is blocked until post-backup suppression and deletion handling are reconciled; searches and offline packs cannot reintroduce it.
- A standing job stalls midway through 400 statements. Restart preserves completed units and the final count reconciles to issued, skipped and failed. Unverified delivery remains distinct from receipt by a member.
- A search outage leaves a manual roster route. The display identifies its timestamp and limits. A delayed payment provider leaves settlement pending until its independent reconciliation.
- An incident is closed only after the service owner accepts restored function, residual risks have assigned owners, and a scheduled retest verifies the primary corrective action.
