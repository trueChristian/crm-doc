# Governing bodies, office terms and delegated authority

A secretary establishes the congregation's constitution, the body responsible for a class of decisions and the rules it adopted. A board may have elected members, appointed members, ex officio seats, alternates and non-voting advisers. An appointment to chair a committee does not grant access to pastoral case files, payroll, bank accounts or a private ballot.

## Establishing and changing a rule

The clerk selects meeting classes such as annual general, extraordinary, council or committee. Each activated class must resolve a notice period and unit (calendar days, clear days or business days with a holiday calendar), eligible voter classes, snapshot cutoff, evidence of notice, participation modes and whether proxies are admitted. Configure quorum as a fixed count, fraction rounded up, or both, with named denominator and required representation, vacancy treatment, when to retest, and the consequence of losing it. For each motion class specify the majority basis, treatment of abstentions and invalid ballots, quorum treatment of recusals, tie rule, mover/seconder requirement, ballot secrecy, amendment and ratification rules. A decision with an undefined denominator or missing tie rule cannot be automatically declared passed. The clerk can record an externally determined decision with the authority and evidence explicitly identified.

Use effective dates when a member resigns, an alternate replaces an officer for a session, two bodies combine or a constitution changes. End the old authority, preserve its historical acts, create a successor mandate and re-evaluate open delegations. The chair can manage the current meeting and propose minutes. A separate authorized officer may be required for confirmation, election certification, an extract or a payment. Access to today's board papers expires under the configured access policy even when the former officer continues to appear in historic minutes.

## Checks the church can run

1. Two seats share the title “elder.” Ending seat 2 leaves seat 1 occupied. On a date before resignation, the historic roster shows both incumbents.
2. A chair grants a delegate a 1,000-unit repairs limit. An attempted 1,300-unit purchase or an overseas travel request falls outside it. A split request still references its common commitment for finance review.
3. A charter adopted on 15 June takes effect on 1 July. A meeting opened on 28 June retains the rule edition assigned to it. A new meeting on 5 July uses July's edition.
4. A non-voting adviser appears on the board roster and receives authorized papers. The system excludes the adviser from voting weight and reports the reason to the clerk.
5. A chart of offices, expiring terms, unfilled seats, rule changes and revoked delegations can be reconstructed as of a supplied date. Publication hides private declarations and contact endpoints.
