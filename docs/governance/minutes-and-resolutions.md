# Minutes, resolutions and the archive

The minute book answers three questions. What did the body decide, who is responsible for carrying it out, and which wording can the church rely on today? Drafts, signatures, adopted records and public extracts therefore each have a defined owner and audience.

## Clerk workflow

Start a draft from the agenda, actual office holders, attendance/apologies, quorum checks, conflicts, motion text, vote results and action owners. Preserve the source values imported into that draft. A later name change or corrected roll has no silent effect on an approved edition. Review restricted anecdotes, identities of children, pastoral information, secret votes and financial annexes separately. Seek approval from the body designated by its procedure. A member can propose an amendment during confirmation. The clerk issues a new draft and returns it to the required signatories.

The approved version has a stable reference, searchable subject, date, body and approved attachments. Record who authorized the minutes and whether confirmation was at the same meeting, a later meeting or through an authorized written procedure. Corrections preserve the original and add a dated erratum or a superseding edition referring to the correcting authority. A change in the body's decision requires a new resolution with its own authority. A later minute correction alone cannot rescind a purchase.

After adoption, assign each action, agree a due date, remind the responsible person and place unresolved items on the next agenda. Use a minimal dispatch for private business. In confidential cases the reminder contains a generic instruction to sign in. A linked purchase request goes to finance with the exact authorization, amount, restrictions and expiry. Finance returns its own request identifier and execution status. Duplicate submission reuses the same resolution/action reference. Closing an action may require evidence and review independent of its owner.

## Retrieval and continuity

Allow lookup by date range, book, body, office, meeting sequence, approved status, agenda topic, resolution number, action owner or completion date. Restrict search results, snippets, counts, downloads, generated summaries, translations and exports using the same record and item permissions. A search for an ineligible closed item returns the same visible result set as if the item did not exist. Provide an offline archival export with approved text, change chain, signature evidence, attachment manifest, opaque identifiers and a legible index. Check archives against the saved digest and report missing annexes. An accessible text copy preserves headings, item order and motion wording.

## Acceptance cases

- A mixed-access meeting contains four general items and a confidential fifth. A member's extract contains only released items and attachments. It reveals no closed item title, speaker name, private file name or document history.
- The approved amount is wrong in a clerk's transcript. A correction records the original amount, amended text, supporting decision and date. Both editions remain available to authorized custodians.
- An outside party needs only the decision granting use of the hall. The clerk issues a numbered signed extract and the release log records who received it and its exact wording.
- A meeting directs the treasurer to replace a roof. An assignee reassignment preserves the old due date and owner. The next agenda shows outstanding work, the finance reference and the unresolved approval step.
- The church changes secretaries. The successor can reproduce an approved minute book, reconcile its sequence, list missing attachments and explain every correction using office-based access.
