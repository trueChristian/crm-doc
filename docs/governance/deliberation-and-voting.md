# Deliberation and voting

The chair works from a timed agenda. The clerk can distinguish a report received for information, a decision by consensus, a recorded vote, and an election. Opening a vote binds it to an exact proposal, eligibility roll, rule edition and quorum check. The chair can announce a result only after the returning officer resolves outstanding discrepancies under the body's rules.

## Decision procedure

Follow the adopted agenda, declare conflicts and test quorum for each item. A mover can revise a proposed motion before it is put. After opening, a substantive revision requires closing or voiding the round with a reason and issuing a new one. Record the outcome of each amendment in the order considered. A passed amendment does not pass the main motion. A tie is reported under the charter's configured rule, including casting-vote eligibility or referral to a fresh round where provided.

For a secret vote separate entitlement verification from ballot collection. The eligibility side records that a right was consumed once. The counting side receives only an unlinkable ballot and round. Prevent timing, export, audit and batch metadata from reconstructing who chose what. Use a minimum publication group size or a teller-certified aggregate when a small subgroup would disclose individual choices. For paper processing, log issued, unused, returned, spoilt and challenged totals with two tellers where required. Store a sealed evidence location under the election retention rule. A secret round can be voided and rerun; individual secret choices cannot be retrieved or amended after casting.

For consensus, record the wording, the chair's recorded determination and any permitted objection without inventing vote counts. For elections, open nominations, confirm eligibility and consent, supply a ballot in the configured order, verify check-in and right to vote, close at the announced instant, reconcile counts, publish the allowed results and transfer the mandate into office administration. A withdrawal after ballots issue triggers the contest's withdrawal rule and records how already cast choices were treated.

## Acceptance cases

1. For a two-thirds rule on valid yes/no votes, 8 yes, 4 no and 3 abstentions passes exactly. With a rule based on all 15 present, it fails. The displayed required count, denominator, abstentions and outcome explain the difference.
2. A trustee recuses from a contract and a temporary chair handles the item. The trustee cannot second, vote on, or certify that item under a full recusal. A later unrestricted item restores ordinary participation.
3. A clerk fixes a typo that changes the authorized amount after a vote opens. The frozen 5,000-unit motion remains intact. A new round clearly asks for 5,500.
4. Two acting tellers report different totals. Certification stays pending, both reports remain discoverable, and a resolved recount records which count was adopted and why.
5. An election with two seats and one accepted candidate can leave a vacancy if the policy allows. No person receives two seats through a duplicate nomination. Secret ballot exports expose only permitted aggregates.
