# Benevolence and community aid

The care team receives requests for food, travel assistance, short-term housing support or a utility payment. The finance team needs an approved amount, fund, payee and evidence of delivery. A church can assist a nonmember, an anonymous walk-in at a food distribution or an outside institution without creating a membership or public directory entry.

## Workflow and privacy boundary

1. An intake worker records requested help and minimum safe contact. A confidential identity check may distinguish multiple people at one address; shared housing, incomplete evidence or a repeat request must not by themselves block assistance. A mobility or language need can guide the service without becoming a public profile label.
2. An assessor checks eligibility and available program resources. A conflict involving an approver, beneficiary, vendor or household routes to an independent decision maker. Show any funding limit and separate a request for more information from a decline.
3. The award authorizes an amount or units and a purpose. Finance accepts a minimum payment instruction including authorization, fund, value and payee. An inventory custodian sees item, quantity and collection instructions. Neither receives the assessment narrative by default. Disposition of evidence follows the relevant retention rules.
4. Track queued, failed, partial and completed delivery. A vendor paid on behalf of a recipient is the payee, while aid counts against the beneficiary and program. A returned payment reopens the balance owed or cancels the unused award according to decision. A successful disbursement must not generate a donation receipt for the recipient.
5. Reconcile awards, consumed commitments, fulfilled amounts, returned money/goods and unspent authority by fund and period. Use anonymized aggregates in reports to the governing body. Referral to longer-term care is a new authorized handoff; receiving help does not authorize newsletters or publication of a story or photo.

## Acceptance

| Situation | Observable outcome |
|---|---|
| A nonmember requests groceries and cannot supply an address | Intake proceeds according to program policy. Stock issue records units once and creates no membership, donor or household assumption. |
| A 300-unit currency award is paid to a utility vendor in two installments of 100 and 200 | The accounting records show the vendor payments, one fund and a total of 300. A third installment is rejected unless a new authorization raises the amount. |
| A payment callback is replayed after a timeout | The original fulfillment and ledger references are returned; reports still show one disbursement. |
| A voucher is issued twice by mistake and one remains unused | Authorized staff record the correction, cancellation and reconciliation; the audit trail preserves both actions. |
| A board member asks for the names of everyone assisted | The ordinary board report returns aggregate amounts and counts with protected small groups combined or withheld. Individual disclosure follows a separate authorized purpose. |
