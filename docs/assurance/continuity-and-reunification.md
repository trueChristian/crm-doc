# Continuity exercises, accountability and reunification

A church may need to relocate activities or account for participants during a building closure, prolonged service disruption or community emergency. Record preparedness, appointments, accountability, family liaison and after-action learning. The church's approved local plan and designated responders direct the response. System recovery, facilities inventory and ordinary check-in each retain their own authoritative records.

## Preparation and operation

Before an exercise, confirm contact channels, backups, accessible assistance, available capacity and how to record activity during connectivity loss. Use fictitious participants or properly authorized exercise rosters, neutral public announcements and a named observer. Coordinate a non-digital alternative so participation does not require a device or a public disclosure of a disability. Staff briefing and expiry of exported papers or devices are part of the approved plan.

At activation, use the current check-in register to establish a roster timestamp, identify late arrivals and choose the relevant anonymous headcount. Accept walk-ins with a temporary reference when appropriate; attempt consent-based reconciliation later. For attendance transfers record dispatch and receiving acknowledgment. A request to collect a person checks current representation independently of co-residence, account ownership or a message confirming arrival. Escalate unclear arrangements to the authorized staff. A person may leave independently only where local policy, age and their circumstances permit.

Use the original event check-out service as release authority for enrolled children where available. Record the resulting receipt in both workflows using the same operation key. When disconnected, the supervisor applies the approved offline procedure, records the accountable operator and arranges a controlled reconciliation. An uncertain or conflicting authority leaves the release unresolved pending authorized review. Display neutral decisions; confidential protective material is never printed on general lists.

At stand-down, reconcile people, anonymous counts, acknowledged transfers, remaining unknowns and confirmed releases. Assign an owner and due time to every discrepancy, return or destroy temporary copies under policy, and publish only approved closure notices. Distinguish a closed exercise with follow-up from an assertion that every individual is accounted for. Recovery of digital systems belongs to the platform owner, while continuity of church activities remains a leadership decision.

## Worked cases

| Case | Expected result |
|---|---|
| A drill tests a contact tree | Every notice is visibly labelled as a drill, goes to the approved test audience, and cannot publish an unmarked emergency advisory. Failed acknowledgments appear against their duty owner. |
| Twenty named participants and three anonymous attendees are expected. Six named children move to another supervised group | Origin shows six in transfer until acknowledged; the combined population remains 20 named plus three anonymous. It never becomes 26 named. |
| A parent shows a previous message while a current collection restriction is active | The system requests supervisor review and records no physical release. The family liaison sees no case details. |
| A connected check-out and an offline reconciliation record the same handover | The organization reconciles the common operation/receipt or escalates a conflict. One confirmed handover is counted; it never makes a second release possible. |
| A worship service moves while the primary communications provider is unavailable | The authorized lead uses an approved fallback with current safe routes and accessible wording. Each attempt has its own acknowledgment and unresolved list. |
| An after-action review finds two people did not receive an accessible notice | Assign a preventive action, owner, retest and due date. Board oversight sees the gap and progress without identifying the people. |
