# Complaints, whistleblowing and grievances

A volunteer can raise an unsafe working practice, an employee can challenge a workplace decision, a beneficiary can complain about a service, and someone outside the church can report suspected wrongdoing. Provide an accessible intake independent of the person implicated. The case owner distinguishes an ordinary service complaint, a workforce grievance, a public-interest concern, a retaliation report and a safeguarding concern according to approved local policy. A single submission can require several independent referrals.

## Control of a case

1. Provide a confidential reporting route, a named alternate and an external/independent route approved by the church where required. A reporter can state it is unsafe to call or send post. Give a restricted case reference and a safe way to supply additional information. Anonymous follow-up must require proof of possession of a private credential, never a guessable case number alone.
2. Human triage decides track, conflicts, independent owner, authority to proceed and information scope. A safeguarding disclosure is handed to its designated owner promptly under its own procedure. Record acknowledgment from the recipient; do not re-interview a person merely to satisfy a general complaints checklist.
3. Explain the scope, next update, support or representation options and controlled appeal route to affected people according to policy. Each receives only materials they are entitled to see. A confidential identity may need authorized handling under local rules; intake must not promise absolute confidentiality.
4. An independent reviewer examines evidence, seeks responses where appropriate, records uncertainties and reaches issue-specific conclusions. A service complaint may resolve by correction at intake if authority and policy allow. Serious concerns continue under the appropriate independent process even when the original submitter withdraws, if policy requires this.
5. Communicate a redacted outcome to each authorized recipient. Allow appeal and preserve both editions. A later retaliation report opens a related but separately restricted docket and routes outside the implicated chain. Non-confidential prevention actions need a verifier and a due date.
6. Deadline calculations use the policy calendar, event that starts the clock, pauses permitted by policy, and the original and revised due times. Record extension authority, reason and safe notice. Expiry triggers escalation, never an automatic decision for or against a party.

## Acceptance and reporting

| Case | Expected result |
|---|---|
| A volunteer reports a concern about the complaints officer using an anonymous route | No ordinary officer sees the submission or notification. The independent recipient can acknowledge and ask for information via the protected reply route. |
| An employee raises both payroll errors and alleged retaliation | Payroll receives the wage-correction finding it needs under its normal process. The retaliation docket remains independently owned and invisible through payroll or staff search. |
| A respondent is entitled to answer a factual allegation involving another confidential party | Reviewer prepares and authorizes a purpose-limited disclosure; source documents and the third party's identity do not automatically travel with it. |
| A complainant appeals a trustee's original decision | The original decision maker cannot adjudicate the appeal. Both decisions remain in the history, and outstanding remedies identify the current authority. |
| A board requests prevention oversight | Provide counts by permitted category, timely acknowledgments and remedies verified. Suppress low-count or intersecting cells that could disclose a report or reporter. |
