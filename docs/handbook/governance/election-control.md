# Secret elections and independent recounts

The returning officer should be able to prove how many eligible rights were exercised, how the votes were counted and which candidate obtained office without being able to prove how an individual voted. Agree the contest, offices, number of seats, candidates, mode, marking instructions, tie rule, count precision, observer access, evidence custody and appeal window before opening. [Deliberation and voting](../../governance/deliberation-and-voting.md) owns the official rounds.

## Separate rights from choices

The eligibility register verifies a person or lawful proxy and marks a unique right used. The anonymous counting record contains a contest, allowed marks and validity. Keep names, person identifiers, signatures, fine-grained timestamps, unique weight classes, device identifiers, ballot return order and correlation keys out of materials that could link the two. Log administrator actions without the individual's marks. Small-batch results or a lone proxy's separate breakdown can reveal votes even when names are absent. Decide a publication granularity that preserves secrecy. Where unique vote weights make anonymity unachievable under the policy, stop and refer for an approved alternative.

For remote or mixed elections, count one live exercise per entitled right across all modes. Issuing a paper right that replaces an unconsumed remote credential must prevent subsequent remote use. If an already-cast anonymous ballot is indistinguishable from others, recovery cannot retrieve that individual's choice. Record the challenge and remedy authorized for the round. Accessibility assistance follows the adopted rules for helper eligibility, confidentiality and confirmation. An assistant must not obtain an additional vote.

## Paper account and counting sheet

**Fixture E1.** Two vacancies, approval vote permitting up to two different unranked names, one unweighted paper per eligible voter, top two candidates win, tie for a boundary seat requires a runoff.

| Account | Quantity | Cross-check |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Blank papers in controlled stock at start | 40 | Signed initial custody count |
| Unissued at close | 12 | 28 issued papers, including replacements |
| Issued papers spoilt and surrendered before casting | 2 | Both cancelled and physically segregated; replacement rights logged |
| Issued but never returned or cast | 1 | Accounted outstanding at close |
| Papers recovered from the ballot box | 25 | 12 + 2 + 1 + 25 = 40 |
| Blank in the box, invalid overmarked, valid marked | 1, 1, 23 | Totals 25. A spoilt paper surrendered before voting is different from an invalid returned paper. |

Among the 23 valid marked papers, 19 have two approvals and four have one, giving 42 approvals. Candidate A receives 14, B 10, C 10 and D 8, also 42. A secures one seat. B and C contest the remaining seat; D cannot enter the tie-break unless the adopted rule allows it. The sample rule schedules a new runoff with fresh entitlements. It cannot select B by alphabetical order, arrival time or a clerk's software sort. If the two tellers obtain 41 and 42 marks respectively, set the count to disputed and recount under observation. A recount changes the evidence and certification of the round, not the number of issued papers.

**Fixture E2.** One seat, ranked ballot, strict majority of continuing valid non-exhausted papers, eliminate the sole lowest candidate each round. Fifteen initial papers give A 6, B 5 and C 4. Nobody has 8. Of C's four papers, two next prefer B, one next prefers A and one has no next valid preference. Round two gives A 7, B 7 and one exhausted. The continuing denominator is 14 and a strict majority is 8. The tally is tied, so invoke the frozen tie rule. If the rule requires a new election, preserve both rounds and reissue once. If a tie occurs for elimination, use the separately defined elimination tie procedure before transferring any preference. Multiperson proportional ranked elections additionally need an approved quota, transfer-value, rounding, equal-ranking, exhaustion, surplus-order and final-seat rule. Selecting the word “ranked” alone cannot certify such a contest.

## Certification and custody tests

1. Two eligible tellers confirm the same physical account, choices and method. Record unresolved challenged ballots separately. If their possible treatment could alter who won, block final certification until adjudicated or the formally permitted disposition is recorded.
2. Certify a seat only when the contest confirms one winner for that seat, the candidate remains eligible and required acceptance is recorded. A person winning an election is distinct from a person beginning a term. Retain an explicit vacancy if an elected person declines and the rules require a new election.
3. Seal count sheets, anonymous ballots and eligibility materials separately, record custodian, access grants, retention trigger and challenge holds. A recount uses the counted version and inventories, captures rationale and preserves both results.
4. Test a result with one voter in a restricted subgroup. The public release may show the whole-contest aggregate. It must not reproduce rare weight, subgroup-by-candidate totals, attendance timing or fine-grained replies that reveal that person's vote.
