# Disposing of motions and weighted decisions

The clerk tracks exactly which wording is before the meeting. The chair can put a valid proposal, refer it, table it for later, accept a withdrawal under the adopted rule or announce a certified outcome. The [decision records](../../governance/deliberation-and-voting.md) carry the governing rule, frozen roll, permitted representation, resolution text and counted outcome.

## Work an amendment

A trustee proposes “Authorize repairs up to 12,000 from the unrestricted maintenance fund before 30 September.” Record the currency, threshold and scope with the text. The clerk captures sponsor and seconder if required. The body accepts an amendment replacing 12,000 with 10,000. A second amendment proposes 9,000. Under an example rule requiring amendments to be decided in order before the substantive motion, put the second change explicitly against the current 10,000 wording. If it fails, the main motion remains at 10,000. If the main motion then fails, the previously passed amendment authorizes no expenditure. If it passes, release only the exact 10,000 resolution to the budget workflow. Link later rescission to this decision.

For an amendment to an amendment, name the parent and the precise text span or full restatement. Dispose of the deepest admissible change first. When two amendments affect incompatible wording, the chair must rule on sequencing or withdrawal. Revalidate subsequent proposed wording against the new revision and seek renewed sponsorship where required. Closing the debate, approving the minutes of debate and voting on the substantive proposal are distinct actions. A timestamp or preferred ordering alone cannot decide an actual question of procedure.

## Freeze and count

For each round bind one proposal revision, ballot method, charter revision, voting-roll edition, scope and quorum check. Prepare the register of rights exercisable by each actor. Assert that every positive-weight right is unique and within the roll; its weight is the adopted exact decimal or fraction. Keep abstentions, blank ballots, invalid choices and absentees separate. Before certification reconcile rights used and options counted under the selected ballot method. A simple single-choice tally must account for all valid consumed weighted rights plus the explicitly excluded or disputed allotments.

Let Y be affirmative weight and B the weight in the adopted denominator. For “at least p/q”, evaluate Y × q ≥ B × p. For “strictly more than p/q”, evaluate Y × q > B × p. A zero denominator has no calculable outcome. Keep exact decimal precision or rational equivalents; a rounded screen percentage cannot determine the result. If a policy states a required whole number of identical votes for a fraction, round that threshold upwards. Weighted rights of 0.5 cannot be tested by rounding them to whole people. A casting vote requires a specific eligible office, a recorded tie, a defined direction or procedure, and a remaining lawful right where required.

**Fixture V1.** A, B, C, D and E hold weights 3, 2, 2, 1 and 1. All five are present. A and B vote yes, C votes no, D abstains and E does not cast. The register has five people, weight 9 eligible, 8 cast including one abstention, 7 decisive and 5 affirmative.

| Adopted rule | Test | Outcome |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Two-thirds of decisive yes/no weight | 5 × 3 = 15 versus 7 × 2 = 14 | Pass |
| Two-thirds of all cast weight including abstentions | 15 versus 8 × 2 = 16 | Fail |
| Majority of all eligible weight | 5 × 2 = 10 versus 9 | Pass |
| At least two-thirds of all eligible weight | 15 versus 9 × 2 = 18 | Fail |

**Fixture V2.** Two rights of weight 1.5 vote yes and one of weight 3 votes no. The count is 3 to 3. Under a simple strict majority the motion fails unless a charter-supported tie procedure changes the outcome. Under an “at least half” rule it passes. If the presiding office is vacant or recused, route to the designated procedure. Do not silently add one to affirmative votes. A proxy casting two separate rights can express different choices if the mandate and ballot method allow it.

## Controls and reproducibility

- The chair sees a zero-vote round when all voters abstain under a decisive-votes denominator. It requires the stated disposition, without reporting a fictitious zero-percent approval or dividing by zero.
- A reconvened round with a newly seated delegate gets a new round id. The first count remains visible under its original roster.
- A member challenges the published 10,000 limit. A clerk can retrieve the original main wording, accepted and rejected amendments, exact final text and certified count without opening unrelated procurement or donor records.
- A correction to a named ballot before the cutoff marks the prior choice superseded as allowed by the policy. A secret ballot after casting cannot be located and changed by identity. Follow the [election procedure](election-control.md) if an error compromises a secret round.
