# Period close and statement correction

## Close authority and evidence

The close coordinator sets entity, cutoff, expected local statement dates, reviewer assignments, outstanding regulatory submissions, signoff date and publication audience. Different milestones matter: a bank account may be reconciled, a subledger may be closed for routine entry, the general book may admit approved adjustments only, and the statements may later be formally issued. A reporting snapshot freezes the posted revision, date, basis, currency, mapping and adjustment set. Changing a draft close date does not change an already signed pack.

| Checkpoint | Required evidence | If a check fails |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Interfaces complete | Last successful transaction sequence or equivalent control per payment, giving, inventory and payroll source; outstanding exceptions and totals | Identify gaps, duplicates and out-of-order events. Reprocess against the original keys; hold signoff for material unresolved gaps. |
| Balance sheet supported | Bank reconciliations, AP/AR aging, deposit liabilities, payroll/tax registers, gift batches, stock value, asset and debt roll-forwards | Drill from control account to its open items; require authorized corrections or documented permitted timing items. |
| Cutoff and estimates | Unbilled receipts, services already delivered, prepayments, depreciation, restricted advances, subsequent receipts and FX measurements | Identify source evidence and recognition policy for each item, deadline and independent reviewer. |
| Statement equations and mapping | Balanced trial balance; financial position, activities and cash flow reconcile; every account mapped at the required level | Show each missing mapping or double-counted line. Preserve rejected calculation and version before correction. |
| Distribution authority | Approved original and restricted/public presentation, reviewed adjustments and meeting resolution where required | Withhold publication until reviewed. Changes after issue create a separately approved revision. |

## Error, revised estimate or new event

A supplier did maintenance before 31 March. In the March close the church estimates 300 supported by a signed completion certificate and posts debit maintenance expense 300 and credit accrued expenses 300. Before the financial statements are authorized, a 330 invoice confirms the same work. Determine whether the new information evidences conditions already present at the statement date and applies to those statements under the approved basis. If the close rules require recognition there, adjust March expense and liability up 30 and ensure the invoice clears the accrual. On payment debit the supported 330 liability and credit bank 330. Never expense both the March accrual and the April invoice.

If a later invoice of 330 arrives after issue and the 300 was a reasonable good-faith estimate based on information then available, local policy may recognize the 30 estimate difference in the later period. If the 330 invoice had been available before issue but was omitted in error, assess materiality and prior-period correction rules separately. A newly occurring April storm causing 330 damage is an April event. Depending on materiality the applicable reporting basis may call for subsequent-event disclosure without recognizing a March liability. The correction wizard must ask for event date, evidence availability, relation to reporting date, statement authorization date, kind of proposed adjustment and authorized treatment. Amount alone is insufficient.

A book with fixed assets costing 1,200 has six months of accumulated depreciation 300 and a carrying amount of 900 under its original estimate. A new engineering assessment changes expected **remaining** life to 30 months with zero residual; a prospective straight-line calculation under this policy is 30 per future month. The first six issued months retain their original 50 charges. If the original 24-month life was inadvertently entered instead of the previously approved 12-month contractual limit, that is an error to assess separately. An estimate change should not automatically generate a retrospective restatement or duplicate acquisition expense.

## Consolidation with a bank fee and cutoff

The national church controls two legally separate books. A transfers 1,000 as internal support. A's bank debits 1,000 on 30 June. B's bank credits 980 on 1 July after taking a 20 external fee. Assume the group has documented that B has a supported unconditional claim at June cutoff and the 20 fee arises on July settlement. A recognized internal support expense 1,000. B's June entry debits transfer receivable 1,000 and credits internal support income 1,000. Reconcile with counterparty identifier, gross internal amount, currency, both cash dates and bank advice.

At June group close, eliminate A's internal expense against B's internal income. The consolidation still needs an asset for the 1,000 in transit pending actual cross-bank settlement; classify that asset per the group's cash-equivalent policy. Where the due amount is external to the group because the sending bank has already been debited and B has a claim on the payment channel, retain/reclassify the in-transit asset appropriately. Where A separately has a payable and B a receivable because transfer was merely instructed, eliminate the reciprocal balances instead, and keep A's cash until it actually leaves. The same underlying funds cannot appear as A bank, B bank and a transit claim simultaneously.

In July B debits bank 980 and external fee expense 20 and credits the 1,000 transit receivable. Eliminate internal support in group revenues and expenses, keep the 20 external fee, and disclose any internal cash/transit presentation bridge according to group policy. Local books remain intact. If B originally booked 980 internal income in July without gross/transit recognition, a consolidation difference of 20 may reflect fee and timing; do not create a perpetual 20 internal variance. Obtain evidence and align book/group treatment with independent approval.

## Reissue and compare

A material prior-year gift of 200 was mistakenly posted as unrestricted even though a valid contemporaneous donor instruction restricted its use. Assume revised comparatives are required under the governing policy. Overall assets and total net assets are unchanged, but donor-restricted closing net assets increase by 200 and unrestricted net assets decrease by 200. Recalculate affected opening balances in the subsequent period, fund release eligibility, comparative figures, sponsor reports and available-to-spend dashboards. Determine whether previously released amounts need investigation. The reviewer can see original and corrected fund columns, reason, authority and the signed release history. Do not send an automatically altered report to members merely because a book has reopened.

Publish a revised pack with revision number, superseded pack reference, issue date, sections changed and an approved recipient distribution list. Recipients see the current authoritative publication and can retrieve or request the original within their grant. An auditor can reproduce both from immutable source postings plus the respective policy/mapping revisions. Donor names, restricted care payments and employee wages remain shielded in general reports. If a tax return or granted sponsor statement used the old figures, open a separately tracked amendment and record acknowledgement when received.

## Close acceptance

A pending bank fee notice dated before cutoff is visible even if imported later. A partial supplier payment leaves the correct aging. A historical FX valuation, stock write-down, payroll accrual reversal or restriction release appears exactly once for the right book. Close can prevent a user with an old form from posting after the lock. A signed snapshot remains readable when a church changes its chart, treasurer or membership access arrangements. An auditor can follow a published number to its journal sources without acquiring unrelated donor, beneficiary or personnel access.
