# Offering room, deposit and donor statement

## Collection setup and separation

The coordinator creates an offering for a service, appeal or outside event and selects the accountable book, campus, collection date, counting team and permitted currencies. Opening a bag records the seal and custodian without inventing an expected total. If envelopes declare amounts, show the declared subtotal separately from actual notes, coins and instruments. Empty envelopes, visitor information cards, pledge promises and prayer requests go to their appropriate workflows. A prayer request found in a contribution envelope is private care information and must not appear in a bank slip or counting export.

The counters record tender amounts, amounts declared on envelopes, anonymous loose amounts, foreign notes awaiting conversion and cheques separately. A count round records who handled the bag, who witnessed, location, time, subtotal per currency, discrepancy and action taken. Independent counts are separate rounds; merging their line totals would double the collection. A correction adds a recount and marks which outcome was accepted. When the counter and the donor are the same person, assign a qualified second person for that portion according to local separation rules. If a rural congregation permits a single officer to transport a sealed bag, record the agreed compensating control and obtain independent confirmation at receipt.

## One collection with a short envelope

Use one currency. The church's two counters open seal S10. These are the only instruments in the bag.

| Item | Declared | Verified | Allocation after review |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Envelope A with a covered donor identity | 120 cash | 120 cash | 90 general and 30 youth, supported by donor instruction. |
| Envelope B containing a cheque payable to the church | 80 cheque | 80 cheque | 80 missions. Original identifying details available only to the donor desk and bank role. |
| Envelope C, dedication blank and no confirmed identity | 50 cash | 47 cash | 47 general under the church's unmarked-gift policy. Record disagreement of 3 against the declared amount. |
| Control totals | 250 | 247 | 137 general, 30 youth, 80 missions. Cash 167 and cheque 80. |

Both counts confirm 47 in Envelope C. The supervisor can resolve the count as actual cash 47 and leave the declared discrepancy 3 in the incident log. There is no missing 3 in the church's bank ledger because 50 was never verified or recognized. A donor acknowledgement cannot state that 50 was received. If a later verified search finds 3 cash in the intact bag, create a supplementary count and allocation, link the original discrepancy and determine whether a second deposit is needed. If 247 was verified and a courier later delivers only 244, that is a different loss after custody transfer. Carry 3 in transit or post an approved shortage adjustment following investigation; preserve both signoffs and bank evidence.

Post verified cash/cheques in transit of 247, credit contribution income in general 137, youth 30 and missions 80. Separate funds receive matching transit debits if fund-level balancing applies. The signed deposit records 167 cash and 80 cheque. The courier and receiver acknowledge handoffs, including seal and times. On bank credit of the full 247 and transfer out of transit, balances become bank 247 and transit zero. Bank and deposit may recognize different dates per church policy; a signed bank deposit slip is evidence of delivery, while a matched statement provides clearing evidence. Record outstanding timing items explicitly where they differ.

## Identification, restrictions and returns

A person later asks to identify Envelope C. Verify evidence and identity privately without disclosing another donor's envelope history. Approval changes giving attribution from anonymous to the verified party, where authorized, and records who approved, when and the reason. The transfer moves no cash and preserves the original offering record. Distinguish a genuine anonymous operational gift, a known donor who chooses public anonymity, and a donor who grants joint statement authority. Personal names on an envelope do not constitute permission to publish an annual donor list.

The bank returns Envelope B for insufficient funds and charges a fee of 5 to the church's general fund. Under this fixture B was a new gift rather than settlement of a recognized pledge. Debit missions contributions 80 and credit bank 80; debit general bank fees 5 and credit bank 5. Remaining bank is 162. Church income is general 137 and youth 30, missions contribution is zero, and fee expense is 5. If the gift had applied to an existing recognized pledge, reinstate the receivable to the extent the claim remains valid. If banking holds cheques in clearing until final clearance, reverse clearing rather than fabricating a prior cleared deposit. The return event links original gift/allocation, deposit, bank charge, corrected donor statement and any pledge reopening.

What if the missions project has already spent the 80? Respect the correction and disclose an apparent or actual fund deficit; do not silently reallocate another donor's restriction to cover it. Route to the treasurer for approved internal support, recovery or plan amendment under the fund terms. What if the bank reports a 5 charge without identifying the cheque? Post a supported bank fee to the proper fund when verified, and investigate linkage separately. A fee alone is insufficient evidence to reverse the gift.

## Statement production and period boundaries

Select the receipt policy, gift dates versus clearing dates, currency, personal/joint identity authority and eligible categories. The system previews current, returned, in-kind, soft-credit, benefit, correction and previously issued portions separately. Donor statements describe what the selected policy permits. Exact official numbering, template, recipient, local address and amounts freeze on issuance. A correction provides an amended statement linked to its predecessor and records why. Returning Envelope B after year end could require an amended prior-year acknowledgement or later-period adjustment according to local policy; the date policy and authorization must be visible. Giving staff can resend the exact prior document to a verified recipient without silently issuing an apparently new valid receipt.

Expose an exception worklist grouped by unverified counts, missing seals, deposits outstanding beyond the configured timetable, unassigned donor instructions, returned instruments, duplicate captures and statement delivery failures. Access to a member directory cannot grant access to that member's gifts. A congregation-level summary can show 167 recognized gifts and 5 general fees after B returns, without naming donors. A donor can receive only the portion attributed under their identity/authority. A volunteer responsible for missions sees the available amount after return and the protected status of any named missionary.

## Acceptance at three cutoffs

1. After count but before bank deposit: counted 247, deposit eligible 247, transit 247 and bank from this collection zero. The count discrepancy is 3. There is one donation posting.
2. After full deposit: transit zero, bank 247, eligible gross giving reflects the three verified portions under policy. Importing the same deposit slip twice does not create 494. A matched statement credit adds no second donation.
3. After return and fee: bank 162, recognized contribution total 167 and expense 5. The missions fund roll-forward and corrected statement exclude the returned 80. Changing attribution for the 47 never alters these totals.
