# Reconcile a benevolence award and hear an appeal

A guest requests 500 currency units for electricity and groceries. She shares an address with an existing recipient. The church must be able to support her without a false duplicate denial, protect her circumstances, approve within the available program, pay the correct party and account to trustees for what reached beneficiaries. See [Benevolence](../../people/benevolence-and-community-aid.md) and [Payables](../../finance/payables.md).

## Assess and approve

The intake worker records the named program, applicant, actual beneficiary, requested support and safe reply method. A vendor or household may be involved without becoming the applicant. Only ask for membership if the published eligibility policy actually uses it; in an open community program a nonmember is eligible to apply. Evidence that two people share a phone, address or surname is a reason to check the relevant program unit, not proof that they are one person or that they already received the requested benefit.

Determine the policy edition and resource limits current for this decision. If aid is limited per household per period, define the reporting unit and period explicitly, account for household moves without revealing another person's history and document the decision when evidence is incomplete. Evaluate the request against program restrictions, remaining authorized allocation, prior delivered assistance, active undelivered commitments and documented exceptions. Keep quantities of groceries and currency for bills distinct. A person who is also a staff member or a relative of the approver triggers the defined conflict route rather than an undisclosed override.

| Responsible person | Can see or change | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Intake worker | Requested support, verification/authority status and safe contact | Cannot release funds or identify other household applicants in an ordinary search. |
| Assessor | Relevant eligibility evidence, applicable prior assistance, suggested amount | Recommends or requests evidence; no implicit payment or donor query. |
| Independent approver | Program, rationale, threshold and material conflicts | Records approval, partial approval or decline and any secondary decision. |
| Treasurer | Purpose, fund, authorized exact amount and currency, payee and payment documents | No pastoral or private family narrative; finance controls release and posting. |
| Stock custodian | Approved items, units, pickup details and delivery recipient as needed | Cannot infer cash award or review household hardship. |
| Board or donor | Approved aggregate expenditure and outcomes | No individual case, vendor invoice naming a recipient, or identifying small-cell report by default. |

## A cash award from authorization to refund

On 2 March the reviewer verifies a 500-unit request in an assistance fund. The independent approver authorizes 350, payable to a utility company, and communicates the partial decision and appeal route safely. Church policy treats this approval as a commitment. It is not a receipt or a completed expense. An extra grocery request for two food parcels is a separate unit-based award. An ordered parcel awaiting pickup is distinct from one physically delivered.

On 3 March finance dispatches 200 against the cash award. The payment service times out. The treasury worker queries the original execution key, sees a successful settlement and posts/links it once. On 6 March a second installment of 150 settles. The cash award is fully fulfilled, reported gross delivered 350. A repeated callback for 150 changes nothing. If the first attempt were still indeterminate, a manually initiated replacement must not bypass the undetermined original.

On 9 March the vendor returns 50. Finance records the actual return against the original 150, references the reversing receipt and confirms fund, currency and bank reconciliation. Net delivered is 300. If the approved authority remains 350 and the amount is still needed, the remaining authority is 50 after the return. If policy closes the award at 300, an authorized amendment releases 50 of commitment. Neither event creates a giving receipt for the beneficiary or recognizes a new donation from the vendor.

On 12 March an independent reviewer hears an appeal on the original partial decision and approves a revised total authority of 400. Record the prior 350 authorization, the additional 50 and the independent basis; do not add a fresh 400 on top of the original decision. Assuming the original 350 remains active, current unfulfilled authority is 400 − 300 = 100. A later payment may use up to that amount after checking need, fund and payee. Any decision to replace a stale payee account requires treasury verification independently of the aid assessment.

## Test the difficult cases

1. Two staff try to reserve the last available 100 of program authority. One reservation/award succeeds or the approver explicitly approves a valid exception; both cannot independently consume the same available amount unnoticed.
2. A 50-unit voucher is issued, voided unused and replaced. The recipient receives at most one redeemable 50-unit instrument; issue/void/replacement remain traceable. Partial redemption and expiry reconcile under the voucher terms.
3. A volunteer delivers one of two food parcels. Show approved two, delivered one, pending one, by the same unit. Keep the monetary utility award out of the warehouse instructions. A returned parcel follows the inventory owner's disposition rules, including suitability for reissue.
4. A case closes while a returned payment is still unmatched. Retain the exception, responsible owner and due date. An aggregate report distinguishes authorized totals, gross payments, returns, net fulfilled and unused/released authority. Never add stock units to money totals.
