# From a private request to a safe act of care

A guest phones after a bereavement. She would welcome meals, a visit and a private prayer group, but does not want colleagues or relatives told about her circumstances. A pastor needs enough information to coordinate the response. The secretary organizing a meal needs a delivery window. The volunteer delivering the meal needs a safe meeting point. Each has a different legitimate view of the same request. See [Pastoral care](../../people/pastoral-care.md), [Visitor follow-up](../../people/visitors-and-follow-up.md) and [Directory publication](../../people/directory-and-self-service.md).

## Intake to handover

1. Ask how the person wants to be addressed, how it is safe to reach them, what they are asking for and what wording may be shared with whom. A request for one private call is sufficient to create a return task even if the person has no church membership, household record, verified email or portal account.
2. Log a care request with source, receipt time, sensitivity, assigned coordinator, safe contact result and review date. Separate the note containing the story from the tasks needed for help. An ordinary welcome task records the handoff acknowledged by the care owner, without exposing case existence to a broader volunteer audience.
3. Agree the actions and a review time. A volunteer's service roster can say “meal delivery, 17:00” and provide approved instructions after the person accepts the assignment. Shared calendars can show an assigned worker as busy without person name, category, address or group affiliation.
4. Ask for separate permission for exact wording, recipient audience, channels and expiry before sharing a prayer request. Record changes and withdrawals. If the approved text identifies two people, establish authority for both or remove identifying details and re-approve the revised text. The requester can decline publication entirely and still receive care.
5. At handover, give the incoming authorized worker only the actions and background needed for their role. Obtain their acknowledgment before closing the outgoing assignment; end the prior worker's case access when their duty ends. Unscheduled absence should have a designated alternate with independently valid permission.
6. On closure, confirm completed and declined actions, unfulfilled promises, any explicitly agreed follow-up, safe retention and future re-entry point. Do not assume closure authorizes a testimonial, annual newsletter, referral or recurring fundraising message.

## Field visibility and dispatch

| Field/action | Coordinator | Meal volunteer | Prayer group organizer | General member |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Person's name | When needed for assigned case | Only approved form of address for assigned delivery | Only the exact approved wording | Only subject's separate directory choice |
| Private family account | Assigned role and purpose only | Hidden, including indirect search hints | Hidden | Hidden |
| Delivery place and time | Approved location and safe hours | Minimal location after assignment and for its validity period | Hidden | Hidden |
| Prayer wording | May draft; person/authorized representative approves | Hidden unless independently authorized | May dispatch only approved text before expiry | Only if separately permitted for their audience |
| Receipts and outcomes | Delivery and review status within scope | May report delivered/failed, no detailed case notes | Dispatch status for its release | None |
| Shared contact endpoint | Current safe decision and purpose | Use an authorized relay when appropriate | No direct address list unless approved | Directory permissions apply separately |

On 4 August the guest approves the phrase “Please remember T. and family this week” for a private eight-person group until 11 August at noon. No newsletter, channel broadcast or audio recording is allowed. On 6 August a coordinator revises the text to identify a particular illness. The revision needs the person's fresh approval; the old approval covers only the earlier wording. A permission named for a private group cannot be repurposed for a wider service announcement, even if all eight members attend the service.

On 8 August at 09:15 the person withdraws. A scheduled dispatch is queued for 09:20. Evaluate current authorization before transmission; cancel the dispatch, remove the authorized live listing and retain a restricted withdrawal receipt. Record whether any distributed copy had already left church control and perform permitted follow-up with its recipients. Nothing in the original request changes the person's directory visibility, email subscription, or a separate meal already approved.

## Outcomes to verify

* A coordinator seeking coverage during leave can see acceptance by the alternate; the volunteer cannot discover the guest's care narrative through search, logs, calendars, receipts, message previews or printable schedules.
* The person withdraws permission for publication while allowing the visit. Only the publication becomes ineligible. Withdrawing all contact cancels future contact actions and routes any independent duty to an authorized reviewer.
* An external agency requests a referral. A reviewer confirms the recipient, lawful or consent-based authority, minimum fields, secure destination and acknowledgment. A disclosure limited to a meal never authorizes a full case history.
* A person declines membership follow-up but asks to resume care several months later. Open a dated new request or reviewed reopening, verify safe contact again, and preserve the earlier refusal for its original purpose.
