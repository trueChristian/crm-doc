# A blended family across two homes

A child, Liv, stays with one parent during the week and another at weekends. A step-parent helps at choir and an aunt collects Liv from a monthly rehearsal. The secretary must answer three different questions: who lives where, which relationships are recorded, and who may perform each action at this time. The household label used on an envelope answers none of the authorization questions. See [Households and delegated authority](../../people/households-and-authority.md) and [Child check-in](../../ministry/child-check-in.md).

## Register the arrangement

Record Liv once. Open one dated residence in each home, mark the church's optional primary designation on at most one, and keep independent correspondence preferences. Record the participating adults once each, even if only one belongs to the congregation. A father, mother, step-parent, adoptive parent, sibling, foster caregiver, appointed supporter or former spouse can be represented with a dated, directed relationship and its inverse. Record only volunteered/useful distinctions; someone may request “parent,” use a single name or decline to describe legal relationships.

A representative submits separate requests for collection, event enrollment and contact edits. The verifier records the requester, subject, action, scope, dates, allowed schedule, source of authority, verifier and any reviewed restriction. The evidence custodian stores a protected order or letter, if needed. The event desk gets an operational decision only. A person who is only the emergency contact is not a fallback collector by default. A child's representative cannot delegate beyond the authority they actually possess.

| Request | Required at the point of action | Outcome if evidence is missing, expired or contested |
|---|---|---|
| See Liv's personal contact details | Verified actor, access to this child and these fields, current disclosure limits | Omit protected fields and household hints. Offer a private review route. |
| Change the weekend address | Authority for the named household or affected fields; review of other persons affected | Submit a scoped change request. Never move all household members implicitly. |
| Enroll in choir practice | Enroll action for Liv and the applicable activity; any additional accepted participation terms | Pending verification. Choir membership or being on staff cannot supply the authority. |
| Receive a reminder | Active receipt authority, permitted purpose, route, quiet hours and safe contact result at dispatch | Withhold or use a separately approved safe destination. |
| Collect after rehearsal | Verified claimant identity, child, collect action, activity scope, active time and no overriding restriction | Neutral “refer to supervisor.” Record who checked and whether physical handover occurred. |
| View a giving statement or pastoral note | Entitlement from that record owner | Household or collection authority supplies no access. |

## Three changes in one month

On 2 May the aunt is verified to collect Liv only from choir on 9 May between 16:00 and 18:00 local time. A relative asks the church to use her number for all family reminders. The clerk declines the bulk change and offers separate scoped requests. On 9 May the check-out worker verifies the aunt's identity and current permission. The worker records the completed physical handover at 17:10. A later search reports a completed release, not just a lookup or advance approval.

On 16 May Liv's main residence changes. End the old primary episode on the effective date and add the new one. If the old home remains a weekend home, record that continuing additional residence explicitly. May 15 reports still show the old primary arrangement. A family envelope approved May 12 retains its historical address and recipient, while future mail is recomputed for the selected purpose. Review current delegated authority separately; a move neither transfers nor cancels custody authority by itself.

On 23 May a parent arrives with an old printed contact sheet. A verified restriction was received after it was printed. The worker requests a current release decision; the supervisor sees “refer” or “deny” and follows the local safe procedure. Neither the parent nor the volunteer sees an alternate protected address or the source of the restriction. The family can use an authorized review route without obtaining protected details through the portal. In a connectivity outage the printed sheet is evidence of a past roster, not a fresh clearance. Record the name of the responsible supervisor and hold unresolved actions for reconciliation.

## Resolve disagreements without leaking information

If relatives disagree on a household display label, scope the dispute to the label; unrelated permissions and the child's recorded rites remain intact. If evidence of authority conflicts, suspend the disputed action and keep a dated decision timeline readable only by authorized staff. A verifier correcting an erroneous restriction records the effective decision and alerts operational owners to refresh. The earlier denial, date and operator remain accurate historical facts. A family photo with three visible people may be displayed only to an audience allowed by all relevant decisions; use a different image when a safe crop cannot be made.

## Acceptance case

On 1 June generate three outputs. The clerk's household report shows Liv once in each qualifying residence and once in a deduplicated person count. The choir's released-child list shows the actual 9 May handover and no release on 23 May. The logged-in step-parent sees only the activity access approved for that actor. Test the same results in direct record access, print, export, invitation suggestions, label previews, family thumbnails and offline reconciliation.
