# Account for people during a disruption and money after a loss

An afternoon gathering moves after water enters a hall. The church needs to account for people, control physical handover, communicate safely, and later document the building loss and prevent recurrence. These are linked responsibilities with different owners and records. See [Continuity and reunification](../../assurance/continuity-and-reunification.md), [Risk and prevention](../../assurance/risk-and-prevention.md) and [Insurance and claims](../../assurance/insurance-and-claims.md).

## Establish the population

The authorized coordinator records whether this is a live activation or an exercise, applicable plan edition, duty holders/deputies, campus, safe information routes and roster capture time. A drill uses distinctly marked notices and an authorized exercise population. A live situation emerging during a drill receives its own authorized activation. The platform recovery owner handles technology service status, while gathering leaders retain responsibility for participant accountability.

At 15:05 the roster contains 26 named people and the door team records four anonymous visitors. Two named late arrivals register at 15:09. The new active baseline is 28 named plus four anonymous. Six named children transfer from one supervised group to another at 15:12. The sending lead records the six transfer references; until receiving acknowledgment they are in transfer, and the combined count stays 28. The receiving lead acknowledges five at 15:16. One remains unconfirmed in transfer under a named coordinator. Counting 28 + 6 arrivals would duplicate the moves.

At 15:24, across all participating groups, 17 named people are present in supervised groups, one is in transfer, eight have a confirmed authorized release, and two have independently departed under policy. The named population reconciles to 28. The door team's four anonymous visitors have separately observed outcomes only to the extent known. An accurate total can coexist with an unresolved individual. The dashboard must never convert four anonymous counts into four verified reunifications or mark everyone located because arithmetic balances.

| Event | Decision and audit | Failure handling |
|---|---|---|
| Claimant presents last week's authorized pickup record | Verify actual identity, current action and scope for this participant, time and current restrictions. Record the physical handover separately from clearance. | A stale or conflicting grant returns a neutral referral to designated staff, with no protective narrative. |
| Parent's message is delivered | Record attempt, delivery and any recipient acknowledgment separately. | Delivery alone cannot move a person into released status. |
| Two workers record the same release from online and offline lists | Match the original release receipt or shared operation key, subject and confirmed recipient. | Different recipients or times beyond configured tolerance trigger independent reconciliation; never silently combine them. |
| Individual rejoins after a recorded departure | Record a new verified arrival event with time and source, preserving the earlier departure. | Update the live count and flag any incompatible overlapping disposition for review. |
| Roster/distribution list contains a protected person | Provide authorized supervisors the minimal accountability and safe contact data. | Do not broadcast the person's identity or location, even if someone claims to be a family member. |

## Stand-down and verification

At stand-down the coordinator identifies outstanding cases, hands them to named owners and records who accepted responsibility. Return or securely dispose of temporary lists according to policy and preserve required event evidence. A single anonymous visitor without a recorded destination remains “unknown disposition” when the church has no reliable evidence, not a fabricated name or clearance. Provide a clear accessible church status update to the approved audience. An after-action review records a primary duty owner, evidence, due date and retest for each failure, including safe notices that did not reach their recipients.

## Trace the later claim

On 17 September three participants report the same leak. The incident coordinator retains three statements against one occurrence. The facilities steward records repairs under the work order and provides evidence of damage. The insurance officer checks the named entity, affected building, policy and endorsements in force at the loss/discovery dates, notice condition and outstanding deadlines. The original people roster and any protected family information do not accompany an ordinary property claim.

On 18 September the church estimates 18,000 currency units of damage. On 3 October the insurer allows 15,000, applies a 2,000 deductible and makes no other adjustments. Record a 13,000 net offer and its authorized acceptance. Finance records 8,000 received electronically and 3,000 paid directly by the insurer to a repair vendor. The accepted amount still outstanding is 2,000; cash received by the church is 8,000 and recovery recognized under the accepted settlement is 11,000 to date, subject to accounting policy. Finance must not treat the direct vendor payment as cash received or charge the deductible a second time. On 22 October the remaining 2,000 arrives: recoveries total 13,000 and gross church cash received totals 10,000. Repair cost, accounting value, gross estimated loss and net recovery have separate meanings.

A new inspection procedure is a treatment, not proof of effectiveness. The risk owner obtains evidence that the inspection is performed, an independent reviewer tests the control, and the board can then consider residual risk and time-limited acceptance under its delegation. If a later retest fails, reopen the finding; earlier completed inspections, settlement and event accountability remain historically intact.
