# Deliver the service that was approved and record the service that happened

A worship planner, musician, interpreter, sound operator and archive editor share the same service identity but work from different information. Preserve the approved plan, live observations and released media separately. The church's rights reviewer makes the decisions governing each use under its agreements. The software can enforce those recorded decisions without deciding what the church is legally entitled to publish.

## Calculate a service order

Take one published occurrence and one named track. Expand its template into an ordered tree of sections and leaf items. Each item has a start anchor or follows its predecessor, an expected duration, owner and audience. A section's displayed duration includes its children but contributes no second copy to the total. A simultaneous activity, such as signed interpretation covering a sermon, uses an explicitly parallel track that must fit its dependency. A handoff cue can trigger at the end of a leaf even when its actual duration differs.

| Item in the planned track | Start | Planned finish | Decision |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Opening, 5 minutes | 09:00 | 09:05 | Fixed service start. |
| Two music items together, 12 minutes | 09:05 | 09:17 | Sound operator sees two item cues inside one music section. |
| Reading, 3 minutes | 09:17 | 09:20 | Reader receives current language and version. |
| Sermon, 22 minutes | 09:20 | 09:42 | Interpreter works in parallel. No extra 22 minutes added to plan. |
| Collection, 5 minutes | 09:42 | 09:47 | Public service order need not reveal private steward assignments. |
| Notices, 3 minutes | 09:47 | 09:50 | A second campus link has a fixed 09:50 handoff. |

The service's expected interval before the link is exactly 50 minutes. A planner changing the sermon to 27 minutes creates a five-minute collision with the fixed link. Present options such as move a flexible item, agree a later handoff with both campuses or reduce an approved duration. An unexplained automatic shortening of a reading fails review. The musician and operator receive newly issued material only after the plan owner approves the revision.

## Publish the right working documents

Issue a stable revision identity and audience-specific views. The bulletin lists approved public descriptions and times. A musician's packet identifies exact song arrangement, key, rehearsal cue and permitted material. A technical run sheet adds microphone, camera, captions, presentation and accessibility cues. A visiting speaker can receive just their arrival and speaking call. Preserve issue time and recipients and invalidate an obsolete rehearsal link when a restricted document is withdrawn.

For each performance establish the intended use, asset, arrangement, territory where applicable, church decision, dates and audience. Treat room performance, distribution of rehearsal material, projected text, livestream and archive as separate uses. A recorded permission for one use does not establish another by implication. When an approval is unavailable, mark the affected output held or omit the item according to a documented editorial decision. The public calendar and unrelated approved materials can still be issued.

During the service the operator starts and completes actual items, records a substitution, marks a skipped item and captures its reason where appropriate. Start and finish observations are effective times, independent of the editor's later entry. If connection fails, two tick sheets can reference the same issued item. Reconcile competing actual times by a reviewed evidence decision. Do not create a new performed item each time a caption team imports the same report.

## Trace a recording from the live service

A Sermon refers to the teaching work; a SermonDelivery identifies the actual occasion. MediaEdition identifies the edited recording made from that delivery. A second telling of the same sermon at another campus is another delivery. The same delivery can produce a complete members-only edition and a publicly released extract. Store segment boundaries against their edition and its time basis, especially where an editor removes music or private testimony.

**Recording fixture.** At recording offset 00:00 the music starts. Offsets 00:00–03:00 are approved for room use and live transmission only. The teaching at 03:00–23:00 is approved for the live service and published archive. A testimony at 23:00–25:00 is approved for members in the room only. A public archive edition includes precisely the 20-minute teaching segment with new offsets 00:00–20:00. It excludes both other portions, has its own transcript review and requires the approved release for that edition. A second edition for a different allowed audience is evaluated independently from those same source permissions. A sold access pass cannot override a withdrawn or pending rights decision.

When a participant withdraws an applicable permission, identify affected segments, versions, thumbnails, captions, listings and downloadable derivatives. Hold or withdraw the affected release as directed, preserve internal evidence, and send the commerce owner the affected entitlement references for remedy review. If the edit replaces an asset, a previously saved playback position outside the new duration resets to a valid point and explains the revised edition. Do not reveal an unpublished identity in a catalogue preview or caption search hit.

**Evidence to reconcile.** Match planned leaves to actual performed items, actual performance to music-use reporting, actual sermon to delivery, edited sources to permission scopes and each release to one edition. A stage manager can certify what happened while one archive segment remains withheld. The media editor sees the actionable decision and scope without exposure to private pastoral reasons.
