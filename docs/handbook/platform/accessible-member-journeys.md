# Commission a member journey across languages, devices and abilities

Test the [inclusive experience specification](../../platform/experience-accessibility.md) with people completing meaningful church tasks. A language switch, failed sign-in, validation error, long table or timeout can interrupt a workflow that appears accessible on its first page. Review the entire journey from discovery to receipt, including printable copies, message links and the route to obtain help.

## Fix the test conditions

Create fictional people with a mononym, a long multi-part surname, different scripts and a shared contact address. Use a family with three children of whom the signed-in representative may register only two. Publish an event in a left-to-right and a right-to-left language, an approved document in one language only and a sermon with captions in the first language. Include one remaining venue seat, one group event requiring all places together and a fee in a named currency.

Recruit reviewers using a keyboard, a screen reader, magnification, touch on a small phone, print and an assisted desk. At least one reviewer should read the target language fluently. Record device, interaction mode, page width, enlargement, selected locale, permission profile and network test settings. A simulation can repeat a condition but cannot establish whether directions or translation are understandable to the intended audience.

## Walk the registration

1. Starting with a public notice, find the event date, local time, venue, cost, available accessibility information and how to contact the organizer. Keyboard focus must follow a meaningful order. Screen-reader headings and labels lead to the same event. The public location of a restricted gathering remains protected.
2. Sign in and return to the correct authorized context without losing the selected event. Provide a usable verification path where someone can use an assistive device or service-desk process under the same identity requirements. Support copied verification information when appropriate. Show timeout policy in understandable words.
3. Select two eligible children. Explain unavailable actions without disclosing a protected child's reason. If contiguous capacity cannot support both children, offer the documented waitlist choice. A reader hears the updated availability and the outcome once. Focus stays at a meaningful point if the page changes.
4. Enter an ambiguous date such as 03/04/2028 into a mixed-language workflow. The system can capture an unambiguous date through labeled controls or ask whether the user means 3 April or 4 March. Store one civil date only after disambiguation. Costs show both amount and currency; a translated thousands separator never changes the amount charged.
5. Submit with an omitted permitted field. The error summary includes a direct keyboard route to the field, identifies what to supply and preserves correct answers. An inaccessible hidden field cannot become an impossible requirement. Read the confirmation screen with amount, terms edition, person and actual outcome before accepting.
6. Repeat the submit action and follow the message receipt. There is one confirmed registration or waitlist entry under the submitted operation key. The message offers the same language where an approved edition exists; a fallback is clearly labeled. A later preference change does not rewrite the language of the original accepted notice.

## Measure the output

| Check | Example release profile | Evidence to retain |
|---|---|---|
| Keyboard and assistive reading | Critical member and secretary tasks finish with no pointer; dialogs and overlays are dismissible and return focus. Status is announced once and every control has a usable label. | Completed task recording using synthetic data and a defect list by workflow step. |
| Enlargement and narrow layouts | At 200 percent text and a viewport equivalent to 320 layout pixels, users reach every necessary control. Dense tables offer an equivalent alternate view where two-dimensional layout is essential. | Test narrow and enlarged states separately, including long translations, modals and validation errors. |
| Contrast and direction | Ordinary text reaches a contrast ratio of 4.5 to 1; large text and essential control boundaries reach 3 to 1. Meaning is available without color alone. Right-to-left text mixed with a seat code or amount remains unambiguous. | Contrast measurements, focus visibility and confirmation of spoken and visual order. |
| Touch and time | Primary touch actions target at least 24 by 24 layout pixels or have sufficient separation or an equivalent accessible action. An expiring hold announces time remaining and the real consequences. | Mobile test near adjacent cancel/confirm actions. Timeout must never charge the user or silently dismiss their submitted outcome. |
| Low-data access | Use a named test connection, for example 1 megabit per second, 250-millisecond round-trip latency and empty cache. Local target for this fixture: actionable event text within five seconds on 19 of 20 runs. | Time to usable text, bytes to first action, repeats and confidence limits. Optional artwork and video do not gate basic interaction. |
| Printed and assisted path | The secretary can complete the same valid action using an approved paper or spoken instruction, preserve who made the decision and provide a confidential receipt. | Original form edition, staff attribution, subject, actual decision time, accessibility of the receipt and disposal of temporary copies. |

## Translate meanings, dates and measures

Keep display language separate from text direction, writing script, physical venue zone, the member's personal zone and the service's calendar language. An evening service shown as 18:30 in the venue's zone should display its date as well as both zone labels when the traveler sees a different date or wall time. For recurrent local times that become duplicated or nonexistent in the chosen zone, the publisher specifies the intended occurrence and the confirmation states the applied rule. A civil birth date never shifts to the previous day when viewed from another time zone.

Plural forms, address formats, name order, non-Latin digits and preferred titles belong to locale and preference data. The untranslated donor or membership status code remains stable under localized labels. A legal or consent edition does not silently fall back to an unapproved machine translation. Offer an identified approved edition or assisted review and store the one actually presented. A text alternative to a data chart includes units, period and suppressed categories; translations cannot expose a hidden cell.

## Decision on release

A critical task blocked in a product-supported language or assistive mode remains open until a corrected route meets the acceptance profile. Record who can perform the alternative, how it preserves consent and whether the church has personnel and hours to support it. Re-run delayed notifications, appointment changes, payment confirmation and cancellation through the same language and accessibility matrix. Publish the supported language and accessibility coverage in member-facing terms, with a monitored means of reporting barriers.
