# Recipients and channel policy

A communication decision combines the sender's authority, the message purpose, the audience, the sensitivity of the content, the receiving person's wishes, current representation rights and the capabilities of the destination. A person's name in a household, on a committee roster or in an imported spreadsheet is insufficient evidence of permission to send a church newsletter.

A communication purpose supplies channel-specific rules for one defined data purpose. Its contact decisions reference the evidence of a person's choice. ## Choosing a route

Evaluate church and domain access, protected-address restrictions, effective opt-outs, entitlement or approved purpose basis, permitted channel, endpoint verification and quiet hours in that order. Apply the most restrictive decision in overlapping scopes, with an explicit dated re-subscription required to lift its controlling decline. An optional message with unknown permission is excluded. A permitted operational notice needs its own documented authority and permitted route. Withdrawals remove queued optional sends before handoff. A household representative may receive a specific child's class reminder within their authority. An adult's giving statement or private care notice requires its own authorization.

For email, use an approved sender and reply route, per-recipient addressing and a plain-text alternative. Handle bounces, complaints and unsubscribes. For SMS, show a preview of segments including translated and non-Latin content, estimate cost and include a recognizable sender and opt-out route where supported. A shared or recycled number must not reveal which household member is a donor or attends a restricted group.

For a Telegram private message the member signs in or confirms another approved identity, initiates a conversation with the church bot and redeems a short-lived single-use link challenge. Bind the specific chat to that person's verified endpoint. Membership in the church's Telegram channel or knowledge of someone's phone number does not establish a direct chat. In a channel, an authorized posting identity with channel posting rights can publish an approved general bulletin once. A permission failure suspends that target and alerts the operator. Private payments, named attendance, child information and personal reminders cannot be expanded into public posts.

For push, verify each device registration, allow removal of a lost device and use generic lock-screen content for restricted topics. For the member inbox, recheck current authorization before showing the detail or a linked record. A printed letter generates a private print job, envelope destination, custody status and a returned-mail workflow. A telephone call requires an assigned caller, acceptable times and a disposition. Prohibit revealing sensitive details to voicemail or another person without specific permission.

## Acceptance cases

1. A household shares one email. A public newsletter can be consolidated only under the household's chosen rule. Two confidential notices are never merged or addressed to the household by default.
2. A recipient withdraws newsletter SMS permission after audience freeze. Revalidation excludes the pending SMS. Separately authorized child pickup messages preserve their own purpose and access checks.
3. A blocked endpoint and a second unverified address produce no automatic send. The operator sees a neutral routing exception. An unauthorized leader cannot infer that safety restrictions caused it.
4. A member blocks the Telegram bot. Future private sends stop after the definitive failure. A general channel post can still proceed under its separate publication authorization.
5. A church running across two time zones can prove the recipient's local delivery window when scheduling, even if the central office was already closed.
