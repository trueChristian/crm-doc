# Delivery and responses

A dispatch can be settled with successes, exclusions, bounces and uncertain outcomes at the same time. Preserve the evidence each channel actually supplies. A service accepting an SMS, an email provider accepting mail and a member explicitly confirming attendance are three different observations.

## Transmission and failure

Revalidate authorization, consent, representation, endpoint validity, quote or spending cap, current source revision and expiry immediately before releasing a queued item. A worker takes a time-limited claim on a single delivery. An expired claim may be recovered. If the prior worker may already have handed off the message, reconcile using the provider's stable message or request key before retrying. Persist submission intent before contacting the external channel. When the provider cannot establish whether it accepted an attempt, mark the outcome unknown and hold it for the permitted reconciliation or a human decision. Do not promise exactly-once receipt across an external channel.

A temporary outage or rate limit uses bounded retries at increasing intervals within provider limits, purpose deadline and absolute cap. A definitive invalid number or revoked device disables or suppresses the route as appropriate. An opt-out suppresses future sends for its defined scope. If there is a permitted fallback, check the new endpoint and classification, perform a separate tracked delivery and link both to the one dispatch recipient. A sender-wide failure pauses its queue. An unknown prior route or private content requires an explicit rule before falling back to another channel.

Only authenticated, context-matched callback evidence affects a delivery. A repeated “delivered” record changes counts once. A late “accepted” after “delivered” cannot downgrade the factual delivery; a later bounce or complaint is retained and reconciled under its source semantics. A read receipt is optional and must identify its evidence type. Treat an email-open signal as an estimate if collected and permitted. Never equate an open, channel-post view count or push acceptance with a member's attendance, vote or legal receipt.

## Replies, print, reports and continuity

Route direct replies to a staff inbox with the originating purpose, permitted content and owner. An authorized operator can answer, escalate, note promised contact and close a thread. Forwarding a sensitive thread requires access to both the subject and destination. Complaint, abuse or safeguarding replies receive the relevant restricted intake process. A channel comment is public input and cannot amend a person's record without identity verification.

Support usable large-print letters, mailing labels, secure envelope preparation, proof of hand delivery, returned post, manual calls and explicit receipt acknowledgements. Suppress private data on labels and list covers. Dispose of rejected proofs, preview copies and old address manifests according to retention and any valid hold. Preserve the allowed evidence needed for formal notice.

The operator's totals distinguish intended people, eligible routes, exclusions, submitted attempts, accepted messages, confirmed deliveries, ambiguous outcomes, replies and costs. Calculate people reached from distinct recipient units with qualifying evidence. Count a public channel post separately. Reconcile campaign totals per recipient once, and report route attempts and retry charges separately. Limit detailed exports to authorized time ranges and roles and omit secrets and unnecessary endpoints.

## Acceptance cases

1. An SMS is accepted, the worker loses the connection, then the provider confirms the same handoff key. One intended send settles. Two repeated callbacks create one logical delivered observation.
2. A dispatched message becomes undeliverable and a verified fallback succeeds. The report shows one person with a qualifying delivery, two routes and the actual cost of each. Future sends avoid the invalid route.
3. A staff member cancels a dispatch halfway through 100 recipients. Already issued deliveries retain their evidence; remaining unsent deliveries record cancellation. An in-flight submission can still return a receipt.
4. A two-language campaign has an individual audience of 50. Two have withdrawn and three have no permitted route, 40 have provider-confirmed delivery and five have acceptance only. There are 45 attempted individuals, 40 confirmed and 5 accepted-only. A separate Telegram channel post changes none of these figures.
5. An operator restoring an old queue after disaster pauses delivery, reconciles external receipts and current withdrawals, then reactivates only permitted, unexpired pending work. An old backup cannot re-enable a withdrawn route.
