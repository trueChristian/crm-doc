# Sermon library and publication

Record the sermon as a work, its delivery as an occurrence and each recording as an edition. A visiting speaker can speak at two services using the same outline, and the public audio may differ from the restricted full-service video. Changing a release window, editing a sensitive passage or withdrawing permission must reach search, feeds, streaming, samples, purchases and staff workflows.

## Publication and retrieval

A producer defines the intended private, group, congregational or public audience, records rights to preach, perform and distribute the material in the intended modes, and selects the service. The librarian imports or records a master, checks completeness and makes accessible formats. Reviewers compare captions and transcripts with the actual edition, redact private prayers or third-party content where required, and authorize each planned distribution. Allow search by speaker, date, congregation, series, language, passage, topic and available format. Apply visibility before search ranking, suggestions, facet counts, related episodes or recommendations.

A live service can publish a waiting page to the authorized audience and switch from live to ended or interrupted. Monitor source availability and actual recording duration. If recording permission is granted, ending a session creates a private candidate edition linked to that service. Public release after a stream requires archive rights, review outcome and an eligible release; these can be preapproved by policy for the precise scope. If only live rights apply, withhold the archive and apply the recording retention decision. A recording failure offers an accurate status and a replacement upload workflow.

Public feeds contain only explicitly syndication-cleared metadata and media. Closed feeds, embedded players, exports, transcripts, captions, previews, search indexes and low-bandwidth audio use the same authorization decisions as direct playback. Provide transcript-only and captioned modes where approved, keyboard-accessible playback, chapter navigation when available, language labels and readable fallback for unsupported formats. A withdrawn download cannot be recalled from a device already holding a lawful copy. Communicate this limit and stop future deliveries.

## Acceptance examples

1. A Sunday evening service includes an independently licensed performance. Its clearance covers live transmission only. The publisher may release an independently reviewed edit that removes that segment, while the untouched recording stays restricted.
2. A speaker changes their public name and withdraws podcast syndication. Update authorized display credit and withdraw the feed entry. Other uses remain available only if the remaining permissions specifically allow them. Historical licence evidence retains the original attribution.
3. A transcript correction removes a child's name. Correct the segment, invalidate old controlled derivatives, rebuild the permitted index and track publication acknowledgements. A removed name never appears in public snippets from stale revisions after withdrawal is acknowledged.
4. A scheduled release is approved for members abroad only in certain territories. If the required territory cannot be established, apply the configured restrictive outcome with a support message. Staff can diagnose the reason without exposing private titles to an unauthorized viewer.
