# Donor service and gift acceptance

A supporter may welcome a receipt, decline all appeals and request anonymity on a public donor wall. A person's capacity to pay, pastoral history, attendance, membership status, prayer requests, safeguarding records and eligibility for assistance must never become inputs to financial solicitation prioritization. Pastoral workers and assistance recipients require a route to report unwanted approaches without involving the fundraiser at issue.

## Acceptance decisions and fulfilment

An offer restricted to purchasing named equipment enters review. The committee checks whether purchase, installation, upkeep, timing and unused money are acceptable. Record the donor's precise terms, beneficiary, decision-makers, conflicts, authorized alternative, date and communication. On acceptance, send approved terms to finance and logistics or asset management. Physical goods are accepted with a documented transfer, cost of handling, condition and receiving location. Finance approves classification and valuation. Declining a gift logs the decision, return instructions and actual custody outcome. A declined or unresolved offer cannot be solicited publicly as secured support. If funds arrived before a decision, finance tracks their disposition and evidence separately until resolution.

One remitting foundation may act on the recommendation of an adviser. Record the verified remitter and any evidence required for finance to identify the correct giving party. Acknowledge the adviser privately if permitted. The designated receipt issuer determines eligibility under the approved local policy. If a company later contributes a separate match it has its own actual gift. A donated service, discount or temporary use of space has its own assessment, restrictions and evidence; it is never automatically included in financial gift totals. A nonbinding legacy estimate belongs to a confidential forecast clearly labelled as such. Avoid any automatic posting, contact with family or public memorial after a recorded death.

Thank supporters in the chosen language with their preferred form of address, an accessible format and a private correction route. A published list must derive from current consent for that named audience. Withdraw promptly from controlled pages and future print runs, identify prior distribution and log what could be corrected. An opt-out defeats a scheduled personal call or campaign letter. A service request or financial document still follows the appropriate separately authorized communication purpose. Store discretionary notes about contact preferences only and remove irrelevant personal information from exports.

## Acceptance cases

- The donor authorizes their name in a printed annual report and declines a public web listing. A portal search, fundraising leaderboard, public slide deck and default web wall hide that name and amount. The approved print edition uses the exact authorized public label.
- A foundation transfers 200 at the suggestion of two advisers. The giving desk records one gift of 200 under the verified giving party. Advisers have two recognition records only where permitted. Actual appeal proceeds are 200 and no adviser receipt is issued merely from those records.
- A supporter offers a vehicle with unaffordable storage and a resale prohibition. The reviewer declines, records custody/return and sends an authorized private response. Finance records no accepted asset or contribution from the offer alone.
- A supporter revokes solicitation consent while two stewards have follow-up actions due. Both actions require a new contact decision, neither makes the appeal, and a legitimate receipt can still follow the separate donor service rule.
- An estate estimate of 80,000 is later distributed as 60,000. Keep the historical estimate private, verify the estate evidence, link the one actually received gift and route the difference for disposition of the forecast. The difference never appears as money lost or a debtor by default.
