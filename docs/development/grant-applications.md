# Grant applications and reporting

A grant coordinator can discover an opportunity, document a go/no-go decision, assemble a proposal, record the sponsor's decision and deliver required reports. The church records the signed grant and its reporting obligations. The accounting service supplies verified costs, draws and disbursements under the agreement and controls financial recognition and reconciliation. A project team owns its underlying outcomes. The proposal and each submitted report preserve the exact edition seen by the sponsor.

## From application to close

Evaluate fit, staff capacity, partner commitments, matching funds, grant term, monitoring requirements and the cost of servicing the award. A documented decision to decline prevents duplicate submissions and keeps the next funding cycle discoverable. Assign proposal evidence and owner deadlines. Compile budget, planned outputs, indicators, match sources, data-sharing scope and an authorized copy of required statements. A coordinator can prepare the submission without authorizing their own financial promises. A submission after deadline needs a documented extension or sponsor acknowledgement; it must not appear accepted automatically.

On partial approval, identify the executable scope, recalculated match, approval authority and unused promises. Create one GrantAward for the authorized signed terms, link the award identifier and schedule the actual AwardMilestones. Share approved terms with accounting where a connection is enabled. Treat reimbursement conditions, restricted-use conditions, reporting deadlines and public recognition obligations separately. Ministry then links the modified ProjectMilestones. If external setup fails, the accepted church award and its reporting deadlines remain in force. Preserve the unresolved exchange and retry with the original source key. A grant manager cannot revise a payout or payment from this queue.

For each due report, obtain the accountant’s approved cost extract or signed summary and independently verified outcomes for its period, reconcile totals on the stated cutoff, compare to approved targets, document variances, explain corrective actions, meet sponsor formatting and language requirements, and submit only permitted evidence. A late/rejected submission alerts the assigned lead with a resubmission deadline. Close each reporting obligation after required acceptance evidence and close the administrative case only after the responsible offices have confirmed the disposition of unspent funds, outstanding draws, disputes and post-award monitoring. Retention and access permissions follow the award terms and governing policy.

## Acceptance cases

1. A church requests 12,000 with 3,000 of its own match. The sponsor offers 8,000 for a reduced scope. Until revised terms are authorized, the pipeline shows 12,000 requested and 8,000 offered. Cash received remains zero. Acceptance creates one award with ceiling 8,000 even if the acknowledgement is repeated.
2. A report states 100 unique participants and 170 visits in a quarter. The coordinator chooses the agreed unit, links the approved ProjectOutcome and excludes named rosters from the sponsor package. An expense already allocated to another grant cannot be counted again above its eligible share.
3. The sponsor returns a signed submission for revision after a cost is disallowed. The current report is marked for correction, the external accounting office supplies reviewed figures, and the revised edition references its exact predecessor. Staff can reproduce both submitted totals independently.
4. A sponsor withdraws an opportunity before submission. The coordinator documents closure and notifies assigned staff. No application is marked submitted, no pledge is recognized and all unreleased credentials and data-sharing invitations are withdrawn.
