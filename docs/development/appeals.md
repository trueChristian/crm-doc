# Appeals and attribution

A congregation raising money for school materials can run a public appeal, invite specific supporters privately and host a benefit supper. Each route may contribute to the same target. Recognition, response attribution and money received answer different questions. The development lead can explain all three without adding them together.

## Campaign decisions

Before launch, approve the use of the church name, public copy, fundraising targets, costs, disposition of excess/shortfall and contact audiences. The campaign manager may load a verified offline response or coded gift envelope; the treasurer confirms the gift and allocation. For a benefit supper, ministry owns registration, an external seller owns ticket sales and the giving desk accepts the separately documented gift portion. Obtain any benefit valuation or event-cost summary from the authorized accounting source. Gross cash collected, gift proceeds, trading proceeds, event costs and net contribution are separately labelled. Record sponsorship transactions by agreed terms. A guest ticket does not automatically create a donor.

Allow last response, first invitation or another declared marketing model for engagement analysis. A tracked click can generate influence statistics, never override donor intent or increase accepted financial allocations. Reversing a gift decreases the current net raised and may reopen a matching balance. Preserve the earlier released snapshot, timestamp the corrected one and restrict nonpublic losses or donor details. If a project cannot proceed, pause solicitation and route held funds or promised support to the restrictions review process for re-consent, alternative use or return.

## Acceptance cases

1. A benefit event collects 1,000 in one currency. Finance separates 600 ticket proceeds and 400 gifts. It allocates 250 of the gifts to the appeal and 150 to another purpose. The admitted cash goal shows 250. Shop sales, gross event cash and individual attendees do not inflate it.
2. An employer offers to match eligible gifts one for one up to 500. Two eligible allocations of 350 arrive under the published chronological rule. They qualify for 350 and 150. A matching promise can show 500 outstanding. Once the employer actually remits 500 it becomes one new verified gift with two match links, never two receipts for 500 each.
3. An envelope is tagged for an education appeal but the donor specifies the building fund. Development flags the mismatch; finance follows accepted intent or seeks clarification. The appeal may retain a response statistic and cannot count the building-fund amount as education cash.
4. A board closes an appeal, then a donor returns an eligible 50 payment. The new current net falls by 50. The board can still retrieve its original dated report and see the correction once. Replaying the reversal leaves both totals unchanged.
