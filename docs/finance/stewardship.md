# Funds, awards and stewardship

A board designation is an internal decision. A donor restriction records external conditions of use. A condition of recognition can delay income even where a project is already approved. A church acting only as collection agent may owe the full balance to a beneficiary. The award reviewer classifies each agreement, documents the approved treatment and selects an effective policy. The classification drives spending controls and report presentation.

## Examples and controls

- An incoming conditional 10,000 award arrives before milestones are satisfied. Under an approved advance-liability policy, debit bank 10,000 and credit refundable advance 10,000. Acceptance of eligible work of 3,000 debits the advance and credits grant income 3,000. The remaining 7,000 ties to the award liability. If the church earns a reimbursement before cash arrives, debit grant receivable and credit grant income at recognition, then debit bank and credit receivable on collection.
- For presentation by net asset restriction, the supported satisfaction of a 400 purpose restriction reclassifies 400 between restricted and available classes using the approved release accounts or reporting reclassification. Total net assets and cash remain unchanged by the release itself. Never count the release a second time as an outside gift. Report the original expense in the dimensions required by the church's policy.
- For a pass-through offering of 900, a church holding funds solely for another body can debit bank and credit funds payable 900. Paying the beneficiary debits funds payable and credits bank. An agency statement displays the held amount and date remitted. A board-designated reserve instead requires evidence of the board resolution and who can remove it.
- Buy 10 units at 20 each, paying 200. If the approved basis measures them at current value, a period-end quote of 22 produces a 20 valuation increase. A separate 5 cash dividend has its own cash and income entry. A purchase/sale changes investing cash flows; a noncash remeasurement does not.

## Acceptance and discovery

A grant manager sees deadline, eligible costs, remaining ceiling, unmet conditions and the next claim date. The treasurer drills from sponsor statement to award, supporting expense and approved draw. A board report can show overseas mission totals without publishing beneficiary identities or destination details. Expired awards prohibit new costs unless an approved extension was effective; late invoices attributable to an eligible period enter review. Closing an award requires final report, sponsor sign-off where required, resolved disallowances, final receivable or refund settlement and a retained record of unspent balances.

When the same invoice of 120 is claimed for 80 against one award and 50 against another, the last 10 is blocked. When a donor waives a purpose restriction, retain the original terms, authorization, effective date and amount released. Reversing an underlying cost reopens dependent claims/releases for review, without erasing submitted sponsor reports.
