# Designations, restrictions and grant obligations

A church can offer a purpose such as meetinghouse repairs, mission support or emergency relief. The giving officer needs to know the undertaking made to donors and beneficiaries. An accountant may represent that purpose by one or several outside codes. Maintain a church designation even where no accounting connection exists.

## Open, use or retire a purpose

1. An authorized church officer proposes a scoped designation code, donor-facing wording, responsible ministry, effective dates, accepted giving channels, instructions on excess or shortfall and whether the purpose can be public. Verify authority to solicit on behalf of any third-party beneficiary.
2. Publish the approved edition to intake channels. Offices, online forms and offering envelopes refer to the same church purpose. A campus selecting a locally named “Missions” code must resolve the correct organization and source date. Pause public intake when wording, authority or beneficiary is disputed.
3. Preserve individual donor conditions alongside any general purpose. Restricted time, recipient, use or contingency can limit acceptance. A board designation and a donor’s restriction have distinct authorities. Private overseas ministry details may require an opaque external code and restricted evidence.
4. On an amendment, retain who authorized it, written terms, effective date, affected allocations and what can be disclosed. Reclassifying an accounting dimension or accepting a different external spelling never rewrites the donor’s instruction.
5. Retire the purpose for new contributions when it closes. A late cheque or recurring payment referencing the old purpose enters a review queue. An authorized officer can seek donor instructions or arrange a return. Existing receipts and previously issued statements retain their original designation wording.

## Sponsor and beneficiary relationships

The [grant application and reporting workflow](../development/grant-applications.md) covers opportunities, sign-off, proposal revisions and sponsor-facing reports. When an offer is accepted, the relationship register records one award with the signed ceiling, term, accountable owner, restricted purpose and milestone schedule. An external bookkeeping service may use its own identifier for the same agreement. Retain the correlation so the liaison can request a verified financial snapshot for the sponsor. Partners and recipients can review only the information they were authorized to receive.

| Event | CRM action | External evidence or decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sponsor offers a reduced award | Preserve the submitted request and offer. Obtain approval for revised outputs and conditions before recording acceptance. | The accepted terms and dates can be handed off for setup. |
| Ministry submits a deliverable | Record edition, responsible person, permitted evidence, recipients, time and sponsor response. Rejection or revision creates a due action. | Where delivery determines eligibility, send the reviewed evidence reference under the agreement. Financial recognition remains with accounting. |
| Church approves outgoing benevolence or a mission grant | Record authorized request, beneficiary reference, amount, currency, purpose, due date and restriction. A confidential recipient may need a restricted correspondence route. | The payment service returns its disposition and authorized confirmation. Only a confirmed outcome may mark the payment as paid. |
| Church acts as collector for another organization | Identify the beneficiary and reporting terms. Show verified collections and externally confirmed remittances separately. Minimize disclosure of contributors. | Accounting classifies the agency relationship and remits or records settlement. |
| Award comes to an end | Verify required deliverables, sponsor acknowledgement, unsettled disputes, records retention and any authorized final notice. | For a financial close, await approved disposition of remaining amounts from the financial owner. A closed reporting task alone cannot authorize spending or return. |

A relief campaign collects 900 for a named agency. The CRM can establish that 900 was accepted for the agency and 600 was reported remitted in an authenticated response. The purpose report shows those two observations with dates. If the finance service has supplied no balance, it is unknown. Marking all funds “available for ministry” would misdescribe the collection. A sponsor report may link aggregate numbers to a protected source without sending aid case notes or naming a recipient.
