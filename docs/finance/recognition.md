# Recognition, deferrals and basis bridges

Choose the church's reporting basis per legal entity and book. Operational obligations, promises, purchase orders and future service commitments can be recorded and aged before they qualify for recognition in the selected accounting basis. A secondary book or report bridge requires explicit policy, control accounts and a reproducible reconciliation. Never sum two presentations of the same entity into consolidated reporting.

## Cash basis fixture

Under an approved pure receipts-and-payments policy, order utilities for 90 and accept an invoice in March. The order and invoice appear in operations; they create no recognized March expense merely by being approved. Pay 70 in March: debit utilities expenditure 70 and credit bank 70. March cash-basis expenditure is 70 and operational outstanding AP is 20. Pay 20 in April: debit utilities 20 and credit bank 20; April cash-basis expenditure is 20. An accrual comparison instead recognizes 90 in March then pays down the payable. Its March bridge from cash expenditure is +20 unmet obligation; April bridge is −20 for the opening payable settled. Both views total 90 across two months. A payment notice is not a second bill or expense.

A cash-basis church can still age unpaid program fees and uncollected pledges as memorandum items, subject to policy. Issuing an invoice alone creates no cash-basis receipts; cash application records income once. Cash received in a donor-restricted fund remains identified by fund. Moving 50 from one church bank account to another is an internal transfer across either basis. Where reporting requires receipts/payments and a statement of assets and liabilities, provide the memorandum schedules and disaggregated fund roll-forward with their own valuation basis.

## Prepaid insurance fixture

On 1 January pay 1,200 for January–June coverage under monthly straight-line accrual recognition. Debit prepaid insurance 1,200 and credit bank 1,200. Each full covered month debit insurance expense 200 and credit prepaid 200. After March, prepaid 600 and cumulative expense 600. If coverage ends on 31 March with a confirmed 600 refund entitlement, debit refund receivable 600 and credit prepaid 600. Collection debits bank and credits the receivable. End remaining schedule portions and retain original contract, termination evidence and calculation. A nonrefundable cancellation or partial coverage requires approved remeasurement based on contract; the operator cannot describe an unsupported expected refund as cash.

A missed February recognition discovered in April creates an explicitly authorized catch-up or controlled reopened period according to policy. Do not post February again when an import replays the schedule. An accrual schedule for delivered utility services estimated at 80 in March debits expense 80 and credits accrued liabilities 80. If the approved auto-reversal is used, April reverses that entry once; the actual April invoice for March service of 90 debits expense 90 and credits AP 90. Across periods expense is 90, with the 10 estimate difference identified. In a nonreversing model debit accrual 80 and expense 10, credit AP 90. A late material invoice may require a prior-period adjustment instead under selected rules.

## Acceptance

A controller can identify open accruals older than a chosen period, prepaid policies expiring soon, contractual liabilities awaiting delivery and a bridge from bank receipts to recognized income. Two simultaneous schedule runs produce one installment posting. A cancelled future installment creates no negative cashflow. The operational subledger total and the posted control account can legitimately differ in a cash-basis view; the report labels the memorandum difference and explains every source. A user with access to a fund summary cannot inspect a confidential insurance claim or employee detail by following a schedule reference.
